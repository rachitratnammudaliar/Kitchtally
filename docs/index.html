Everything it does, offline.

Made for everyday use: a few taps, clear numbers, nothing to set up.

🥖

% left

Bread, oil and ketchup as 100, 75, 50 or 25% left, not "200 ml". Pieces, packets, grams, kg, ml and litres too.

Meals left

"5 onions, about 8 meals." Cook a meal takes one meal from everything you used in one tap, and it turns red at the last meal.

Almost out

A quiet line under the item when it's at 25% or your own low point. No notifications.

Download Kitchtally

Free. Get the latest version from GitHub. It isn't in an app store yet, so your phone asks you to confirm the first time. Updates install over the old version and keep your data.

Android 8.0 and newer

  1. Open the latest release on your phone and tap Kitchtally-…-Android.apk.
  2. Open the file. If asked, allow your browser or Files app to install unknown apps.
  3. If Play Protect warns about an unknown developer, tap More details → Install anyway.
  4. Samsung Galaxy: if it's blocked, turn off Auto Blocker in Security and privacy, install, then turn it back on.

Get the Android app

iPhone and iPad, iOS 16 and newer

  1. Download Kitchtally-…-iOS.ipa from the latest release on a Windows PC or Mac.
  2. Install it with Sideloadly or AltStore and your Apple ID.
  3. On the iPhone: Settings → General → VPN & Device Management, trust your Apple ID, and turn on Developer Mode.
  4. With a free Apple ID, refresh it every 7 days (AltStore does it for you).

Get the iPhone file

Switching phones? Android and iPhone read the same backup file: Settings → Export on one, Import on the other.

Your data never leaves your phone

No servers to trust, because there aren't any. Read the privacy policy.

No internet

The app has no internet permission at all.

Only on your phone

Items, prices and photos stay in the app's private storage.

No cloud copies

Excluded from Android cloud backups.

No account, ads or tracking

Open it and start. Nothing to sign up for.

Built with Kotlin, Jetpack Compose and Room on Android; Swift and SwiftUI on iPhone, with the same logic and the same tests on both. Every change is tested automatically on GitHub before a release.
